# Reading guide evidence and delivery

## Ten guides, September 11, 2026

The ten planned subjects are implemented: caffeine, supplements, energy choices, sleep, exercise, vaccines, nutrition, climate attribution, evolution, and interpreting medical evidence. All ten were publicly accepted in v1.17.0 at `d64fe7cf170a2f4d65a7bc6d0dfd94f0f23e5918` on September 11, 2026. The release records exact public identity, complete guide bodies/citations/metadata, 50 discovery pages, sitemap and unknown-route checks. This completes the guide milestone, not the broader reader-experience goal; see [reader-experience-goal.md](reader-experience-goal.md) for the current state of the remaining requirements.

Guide narratives live alongside the existing frontend explainers, in `front-end/src/data/reading-guides/`. They synthesize and link to the canonical API-backed claim reviews; they do not create duplicate claim records or inflate the 750-claim count. Small discovery metadata is separate from lazily loaded article bodies. Dates are explicit source-check dates, not fabricated expert-review dates or database restart timestamps.

## Evidence checks

- Caffeine: Consensus Pro discovery and fetched records were cross-checked against primary publication abstracts in PubMed/Europe PMC. Carvalho 2022 (PMID 35536449, 60-study synthesis), Khodadadi 2025 (PMID 39905628, 80 initially inactive men), Gardiner 2023 (PMID 36870101, 24-study sleep synthesis), and Juliano/Griffiths 2004 (PMID 15448977, withdrawal review). FDA adult safety guidance was read directly. Abstract-only review is disclosed; no full-text review or systematic new literature search is claimed. Between-study habitual-use comparisons and longitudinal habituation are distinguished. No universal caffeine-reset duration, dosing escalation, or individualized sleep-loss estimate is recommended.
- Supplements: FDA regulatory questions and answers; NIH Office of Dietary Supplements' general consumer and exercise-performance fact sheets; USPSTF's 2022 vitamin/mineral recommendation and clinician summary. These were checked for population, purpose, product-quality limits, and safety context. Prevention findings do not replace deficiency treatment or pregnancy guidance. Creatine evidence is outcome- and population-specific. No brand, purchase, or personal regimen is recommended.
- Energy: IPCC AR6 WGIII Chapter 6 energy-system/storage/transmission material; NREL's 2021 lifecycle update; IEA's 2024 integration overview, framework, and executive-summary findings; the abstract of Markandya/Wilkinson 2007 (PMID 17876910). The historical and largely European health evidence is explicitly qualified. The storage arithmetic is labeled hypothetical and excludes losses; no current universal price or mortality ranking is invented.
- Sleep: CDC adult sleep-duration and quality guidance; NHLBI insomnia treatment and sleep-apnea symptoms; primary indexed abstracts of Ruan 2025 (PMID 40449065, 42 trials/4,245 adults), Scott 2025 (PMID 40982264, 67 trials/5,232 participants), and the final AASM combination-treatment guideline (PMID 41975142, 2026). The earlier Consensus discovery notes were checked against Europe PMC. Sleep hygiene and structured CBT-I are distinguished. The AASM recommendations compare combination treatment separately against medicine alone and CBT-I alone; both are conditional with low-certainty evidence. No medication change or self-prescribed sleep-restriction protocol is offered.
- Exercise: CDC adult targets and intensity explanations; WHO physical activity guidance; Ding 2025 (PMID 40713949, 57 studies/35 cohorts, 31 studies in meta-analyses) discovered through one Consensus Pro search and checked against the primary indexed abstract. The publisher's correction (DOI 10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00199-9) identifies supplementary table 8; the guide links the correction without reproducing that table. Observational associations, confounding, activity accessibility, and the limits of step-only comparisons are explicit.
- Vaccines: Cochrane's 2021 MMR/MMRV public summary and abstract; FDA VAERS explanation; Hviid 2019 primary abstract (PMID 30831578, 657,461 children); WHO's December 2025 vaccine-safety committee statement. The measles effectiveness estimates are those in the Cochrane review, not a universal current schedule estimate. Febrile seizures and the rare platelet disorder are distinguished from autism allegations. A reported event is not automatically a causal excess; the denominator exercise is invented, not vaccine surveillance data.
- Nutrition: WHO's January 2026 healthy-diet principles; Cochrane's long-term saturated-fat review; the revised 2018 PREDIMED abstract (PMID 29897866), not the withdrawn 2013 report; Hall's 2019 inpatient trial abstract (PMID 31105044). PREDIMED's randomization departures and supported high-risk population remain visible. Hall's small, short, whole-menu intervention is not used to infer every additive's effect or decades of disease risk; the indexed record's corrections are acknowledged. Cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality are not conflated.
- Climate attribution: IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report's official indexed statement A.1, with the 2011-2020 versus 1850-1900 periods retained; NASA's solar-observation and atmospheric-temperature explanation; Met Office's attribution-method page and its 2023 discussion of rapid-study, observation, model, and geographical limitations. Direct IPCC rendering was intermittent; no full-chapter reading is claimed. A NOAA page returned a browser-verification screen and the National Academies page did not supply the needed methods text, so neither is presented as a checked guide source. The Met Office pages supplied the actual event-attribution explanation. Probability arithmetic is explicitly hypothetical, not an analysis of a current disaster.
- Evolution: UC Berkeley's inherited-change and misconception explanations; Smithsonian Human Origins' genetics and evidence overviews; CDC's January 2025 antimicrobial-resistance mechanism explanation. Shared ancestry is not descent from living chimpanzees, DNA percentages depend on the comparison, and selection and drift are distinct. The guide uses complementary evidence rather than claiming to reconstruct individual fossil lineages.
- Medical evidence: Cochrane Handbook chapters 13, 14, and 15, specifically selective non-reporting, outcome-specific GRADE, applicability, precision, and effects; FDA surrogate-endpoint definitions and validation categories. The two absolute-risk examples are original arithmetic, not trial data. Statistical precision does not include every source of uncertainty; GRADE is not a percentage of scientists, and funnel asymmetry is not proof of publication bias.

Paragraph citation IDs point to the source list. Source entries explain the kind of evidence and its limitations. Original reading exercises are labeled as such instead of being presented as experimental findings. This is a curated synthesis, not a new systematic review or a claim of independent expert review.

The NREL report's old `www.nrel.gov` address failed DNS during the link check. The laboratory's current research catalog identifies the same 2021 report at `https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy21osti/80580.pdf`; that PDF returned HTTP 200. The guide uses the working institutional document URL while preserving the report's original attribution and year.

## Verification scope

- Guide tests check all ten named subjects, discovery uniqueness, valid claim/topic links in both directions, source anchors, HTTPS sources, explicit review scope, substantive content, future dates, important evidence qualifications, accessibility-route coverage, and rejection of unknown/prototype-property slugs.
- The built-browser smoke checks every guide's body paragraphs, canonical and Article metadata (including exact citation URLs), citations/contents anchors, mobile widths and text enlargement, and backend independence. Each guide has a real review-to-guide-to-review navigation check. Every connected review and topic is checked for server-rendered guide discovery; Explainers, browser history, unknown-guide 404s, and the backend-independent sitemap are also covered. The API fixture uses the real source catalog; it is not production database integration.
- All ten detail pages and the guide index are included in the light/dark accessibility checks.
- Source correctness requires editorial review of the cited material. Passing structural tests or a minimum word count is not proof of scientific accuracy.
- Release acceptance also requires CI and checks of the public release identity, guides, connected review links, and sitemap. Release publication alone is not deployment proof.

## Local acceptance, September 11, 2026

The complete ten-guide tree passed root `npm ci`, install-script policy and native-lock verification, frontend/backend lint, typecheck, tests, and production build. The backend suite includes 154 passing tests and a 100/100 covered-question top-three search result on the development benchmark, with separate unsupported-query checks. This is not a blind evaluation or a visitor-success measurement.

The built-browser reading/search suite passed for all ten guides, including exact article paragraphs and citation metadata, 390px and 320px widths, 200% text, and every guide's review-to-guide-to-review journey. All 32 accessibility routes passed in both light and dark modes. Backend runtime, SSR public assets, and SSR route-rule checks passed. Visual inspection confirmed the article remains a single continuous column after text enlargement is reset; the screenshot harness waits for font/layout repaint before full-page capture.

These local results are distinct from public acceptance. [v1.17.0 release notes](https://github.com/anderson-webops/isthereconsensus.org/releases/tag/v1.17.0) record passed PR #55 and merged-main CI, followed by exact-commit public checks for all ten guides.
