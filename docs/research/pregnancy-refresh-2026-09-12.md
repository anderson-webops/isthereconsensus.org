## Substantive changes

Replace the blanket safety statement with an outcome-specific synthesis. Keep
the generally favorable pregnancy benefit-risk conclusion while explaining
observational limitations, rare outcomes, historical formulations and differences
between absolute benefit, relative associations and guidance. Add 2025-2026
first-trimester studies, with their actual older exposure periods. Retain a
reported postpartum-bleeding association alongside a larger comparison instead
of omitting contrary evidence. Separate neonatal outcomes, longer-term
development, antenatal infant protection and antibodies measured in breast milk.

The attributed CDC and ACOG recommendations replace generic institutional
anchors that did not identify an assessed document. Their recommendations are
not counted as independent experiments. Neither the existing agreement score
nor the number of citations is presented as a measured percentage of experts.

## Quantitative interpretation

- Ciapponi includes 177 studies and 638,791 participants in 41 countries, not all
  in each pooled comparison. The pooled 72% relative reduction in severe maternal
  disease or hospitalization (95% CI, 42%-86%) is historical and is not a current
  absolute benefit for an individual.
- Bernard includes 527,564 live-born infants, 130,338 exposed in the first
  trimester. Major-malformation prevalence was 176.6 versus 179.4 per 10,000;
  weighted odds ratio 0.98 (0.93-1.04). Live-birth selection, coding, exclusions
  and unmeasured confounders matter. Rare subtype intervals do not prove absence
  of meaningful risks; nominal protective associations among many tests are not
  promoted as causal benefits.
- Madni includes 12,097 registry pregnancies. The cumulative miscarriage estimate
  from 6 to 20 weeks was 10.79% (10.01%-11.56%), using exposure-timed entry and
  censoring. It is not a vaccine-attributable rate or a crude ratio of losses to
  all pregnancies. Selection, enrollment after loss, self-report and historical
  comparisons constrain interpretation. The Moderna-versus-Pfizer comparison
  is not a vaccinated-versus-unvaccinated estimate.
- Dick reported postpartum bleeding in 9.5% of third-dose versus 3.5% of
  two-dose recipients, adjusted odds ratio 3.34 (2.07-5.39). The third-dose versus
  unvaccinated adjusted odds ratio was 3.88 (2.41-6.25), not the risk ratio used
  in the later review's prose. Ontario included 32,689 births from 32,125
  pregnancies, with 18,491 third-dose births; adjusted hazard ratio 1.01
  (0.89-1.16). The populations, comparators, timing and estimands differ. The
  larger study does not erase the smaller signal or prove zero risk.
- Norman includes 196,470 infants from 193,753 pregnancies, with 94,303 exposed
  infants. Neonatal mortality was 0.9 versus 1.8 per 1,000, adjusted odds ratio
  0.68 (0.50-0.91). Residual confounding and multiple comparisons prevent a
  universal causal mortality claim. The abstract's significant hypoxic-ischaemic
  estimate applies to second-trimester exposure, not the overall combined
  outcome. Overall respiratory distress was borderline (1.05, 1.00-1.11), so the
  synthesis does not claim every outcome was exactly null or protective.
- Antibodies detected in breast milk, transient mRNA detection and clinical
  infant hospitalization after antenatal vaccination answer different questions.
  The review does not translate one into a measured benefit or harm of another.

conflicts. Prasad authors report committee and pregnancy-trial roles, including

research grants are disclosed. Bernard reports no competing interests; a

and no known competing interests. Dick reports no funding or competing interests.
Lactation-review disclosures were inaccessible. These facts are not reduced to
an unsupported blanket declaration of independence, and do not determine validity
by themselves.

This is a targeted narrative source assessment, not an exhaustive systematic
review, independent GRADE assessment or expert approval. Overall moderate
certainty is an explicitly identified editorial judgment. The six individual
evidence summaries are unrated, and every source remains `not_appraised` under
the formal appraisal field. Existing scientific review dates and expert approval
state are preserved.
