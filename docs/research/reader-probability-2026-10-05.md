## Coverage audit and reader value

The complete baseline catalog was searched in titles, bottom lines, stable cores
and editor summaries, rather than titles alone. No baseline text matched the
proposition-specific terms Simpson, standard error, percentage point, expected
value, probability density, gambler, uncorrelated, missing data, histogram,
bin width, standard deviation, conditional probability or percent change.
Broader searches of average, median and independence found application summaries
and funding or personal-independence contexts, not canonical answers to these
mathematical propositions. Existing significance, confidence-interval, sample
selection and correlation-causation reviews were inspected as neighbors and
are preserved, not renamed or counted again. This selection is an editorial
gap audit, not invented reader submissions, popular demand or an expert survey.

The four homes distinguish separate questions:

- Averages and data displays: mean/median, pooled means, Simpson reversal,
  unknown-versus-zero values and histogram grouping.
- Percentages and conditioning: percentage points, reversal of equal percent
  changes, compounded changes, reversed conditional probabilities and overlap.
- Independence and probability models: disjointness, without-replacement
  sampling, null events, density height and independent-trial convergence.
- Expectation, spread and sampling uncertainty: attainable outcomes, nonlinear
  dependence, variance units, estimator precision and normal-model coverage.

Each review has a different bottom line and at least 200 question-specific body
words. Independently constructed nonclinical examples expose the distinction
instead of relying on generic qualification text. Neither application examples,
guides, old refreshes nor comparisons are counted as new canonical reviews.
The frozen unfamiliar-query evaluation remains unopened and separate from
the public terminology diagnostics used to verify findability.

## Interpretation and evidence boundaries

Penn State lessons share institution/course provenance, NIST sections share a
handbook and StatLect pages share an author. They do not become independent
experiments when cited together. These are method-defining references, not
formal scientific consensus declarations, clinical guidelines or expert votes.
Appraisal remains `not_appraised`; study design remains `not_coded`; no expert
review identity is invented. The existing legacy confidence value is editorial,
not a measured fraction of researchers agreeing. No actual sample, apparatus,
visitor dataset or unpublished private request was analyzed.

Key boundaries are explicit: positive conditional denominators; null events;
countable versus uncountable probability operations; finite moments and positive
variances; specified sampling designs; density units and areas; sample versus
population variance; normal versus merely symmetric distributions; and causal
adjustment versus aggregation arithmetic. Model precision does not fix selection
bias or measurement error. An unknown value is not an observed zero; omitting
incomplete records is not a universally unbiased alternative. The normal-rule
counterexample uses an inclusive interval. No clinical, financial, gambling or
biological-procedure recommendation is provided.

The new records use stable literal announcement IDs and the real source-check
cutoff. Prior cohort research dates are unchanged. Original reference checks do
not certify an exhaustive correction or integrity audit. Funding and conflicts
across teaching records were not exhaustively audited.
