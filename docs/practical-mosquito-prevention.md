## Reader experience and new coverage

`/compare/mosquito-bite-prevention` compares six approaches across evidence for
protection and coverage/use limits. Its twelve qualitative findings keep their
sources, endpoints and applicability together. Personal infection prediction
and child-product selection contexts withhold findings rather than borrowing
general evidence. No universal ingredient ranking, personal treatment plan or
expert-agreement percentage is calculated.

Five new reviews cover concentration versus duration, registered OLE/PMD versus
essential oil, wristband delivery, laboratory versus field protection time,
and supplementary indoor spatial emanators. These are distinct from the
existing bed-net review, which remains intact and is recorded as adjacent
coverage in the gap registry. One curated collection and a substantial guide
connect the new questions. Shared registries provide topic/review/search/Ask
discovery, sitemap, library saving, substantive history and private feedback.

## Source verification

Checked September 12, 2026 UTC using primary guidance and published evidence:

- [CDC Yellow Book](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/mosquitoes-ticks-and-other-arthropods.html),
  2026 edition published April 2025: topical efficacy, formulation, duration,
  clothing, precautions and child-label sections. Its older general spatial
  disease-evidence statement does not override later WHO indoor guidance.
- EPA [safe use](https://www.epa.gov/insect-repellents/using-insect-repellents-safely-and-effectively),
  [DEET](https://www.epa.gov/insect-repellents/deet) and
  [treated clothing](https://www.epa.gov/insect-repellents/repellent-treated-clothing):
  relevant full sections checked. Preserve the limited OLE-only child-label
  exception, covered-skin limits and formulation dependence. Historical
  surveillance numbers and stale registration-review deadlines are not
  presented as current facts.
- Colucci and Müller, [DOI 10.1038/s41598-018-30998-2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-30998-2):
  publisher PDF methods, results, limitations and disclosures checked; Figure 4
  visually checked. Eighteen enrolled, seventeen analyzed, two missing field
  measurements. Endpoint is first landing, with six-hour field censoring and
  low field landing pressure. The abstract reverses two PMD species medians
  relative to results/Figure 4. Those numbers are withheld, not silently
  corrected. No correction was located in the targeted check, which is not an
  exhaustive integrity review. Public funding, formulation supply and the
  authors' competing-interest declaration are separately recorded.
- Rodriguez et al., [DOI 10.1093/jisesa/iew117](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5388317/):
  primary full-text methods, tables, results and discussion checked. Two
  selected volunteers, four replicates per treatment/time point, short
  attraction assay. Label hours are not measured duration. No numerical brand
  ranking is reproduced or supplementary statistical reanalysis claimed.
  Funding/COI statements were not located; this is not asserted independence.
- [WHO malaria guideline](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/guidelines-for-malaria):
  current September 10, 2026 PDF, 494 pages. Recommendation p. 86, evidence to
  decision pp. 87–92 and evidence tables/footnotes pp. 346–348 checked. The
  recommendation and table were visually inspected. This replaces relying on
  a cached August 2025 landing-page result. Underlying trials and the
  unpublished systematic review were not independently reappraised.

One Consensus search returned nineteen records; five were fetched for
discovery. No Deep Review was used. Primary verification determined inclusion.
The older Fradin trial, inaccessible full picaridin review, corrected 2026
descriptive synthesis and Ghana formulation trial were considered but not used
as numerical support. The latter's questionable test-statistic/probability
pairs and formulation-specific landing results were not promoted into a
general efficacy claim. This is targeted AI-assisted research, not an
exhaustive systematic review or independent expert approval.

## Numerical and causal boundaries

WHO reports an overall infection-incidence rate ratio of 0.77 (95% CI
0.56–1.05; 7,002 participants, four trials; low certainty) and a subgroup at
at least 80% household coverage of 0.67 (0.56–0.81; 2,878 participants, two
trials; high certainty). The recommendation is conditional with moderate
overall certainty. These labels refer to different assessments and must not
be collapsed. The 80% split was selected in advance, not established by
randomizing coverage or demonstrating a biological threshold. No absolute
benefit, mortality benefit or cross-study pooled estimate is calculated.
